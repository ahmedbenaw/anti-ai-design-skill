## Tells register

### T1. The indigo/purple-to-violet gradient
- **What it looks like:** Blue-to-purple (often purple-to-cyan or purple-to-pink) gradient behind the hero, on CTAs, or as page background; frequently on dark backgrounds with glowing blobs.
- **In code:** `bg-gradient-to-r from-purple-500 to-pink-500`, `from-indigo-500 to-purple-600`, `linear-gradient(135deg, #4f46e5 0%, #a855f7 100%)`; hex range #6366F1–#8B5CF6 (Tailwind indigo-500 → violet-500).
- **Tools named:** Claude/Claude Sonnet (9), Claude Code, Cursor, v0, Windsurf (2, 12), Figma Make, Lovable (23); root cause traced to Tailwind's `bg-indigo-500` default (6, 9, 14, 17).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 23
- **Quotes:** "Traces directly back to Tailwind's indigo-500 default and the training-data feedback loop" (3). "Purple/violet gradients and cyan-on-dark are the most recognizable tells of AI-generated UIs" (16). "He set the default UI color to bg-indigo-500… that would define the aesthetic of an entire generation of AI" (9).
- **Counter-evidence:** Humans did it first: "Notion, Linear, Vercel and similar influential products used these gradients" (7); purple gradients dominated 2015–2020 human web design — Stripe, Instagram, Twitch (8). So a purple gradient alone is not proof of AI.

### T2. "VibeCode purple" lavender accent
- **What it looks like:** A specific lavender-purple accent shade (~#B794E2) recurring across generated UIs and AI imagery.
- **In code:** `bg-purple-400`, `text-purple-300`, shadcn default token overrides.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code, image-generation models (1).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 1
- **Quotes:** "A specific shade of lavender-purple that leaks out of most image generation and prompts" (1).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T3. Default dark mode with glow aesthetics
- **What it looks like:** Permanent dark theme (#0f0f0f-ish), medium-grey body text, neon/colored glows, "aurora" backgrounds; the crypto/gaming/AI-launch look.
- **In code:** dark bg + `color: #888`, `box-shadow: 0 0 20px rgba(purple,.5)`, radial-gradient halos.
- **Tools named:** LLM defaults generally (1); training data from gaming/crypto/AI launch sites (7). Developers Digest measured permanent dark theme as the single most common tell (34% of scanned pages).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 1, 7, 12, 15, 16, 19
- **Quotes:** "Dark mode with purple accents is the default aesthetic LLMs reach for" (1). "Dark backgrounds with colored box-shadow glows are the default 'cool' look of AI-generated UIs" (16). "If the page cannot answer those without the glow, design carries too much weight" (19).
- **Counter-evidence:** Dark mode itself is legitimate: "Dark mode earns depth through typography, contrast, and thoughtful use of surface levels" (7) — the tell is the glow decoration, not darkness.

### T4. Radial spotlight / glow halo backgrounds
- **What it looks like:** A faint saturated radial haze behind hero or sections acting as a fake spotlight; aurora-borealis bloom behind headlines.
- **In code:** `background: radial-gradient(...)` with saturated accents at low alpha; absolutely positioned blurred divs (`blur-3xl opacity-30`).
- **Tools named:** generated-UI shortcut generally (16); vibe-coded UIs (7).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 7, 16
- **Quotes:** "A saturated radial glow on a dark page is a familiar generated-UI shortcut" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found; Linear-style human sites also use these (implied by 7's origin story).

### T5. Gradient text
- **What it looks like:** Headline words filled with a gradient instead of a solid colour.
- **In code:** `bg-clip-text text-transparent bg-gradient-to-r ...`.
- **Tools named:** general LLM output (16); part of the "hero with a gradient headline" template (11); AI-built sites per West (6).
- **Category:** typography / colour
- **Sources:** 6, 11, 16
- **Quotes:** "Gradient text is decorative rather than meaningful. A common AI tell" (16). "Hero with a gradient headline, three feature cards in a row" (11).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T6. Barely-passing (or failing) text contrast
- **What it looks like:** Grey-on-dark body text under WCAG AA; grey text on coloured backgrounds looking washed out.
- **In code:** contrast ratio < 4.5:1; `text-gray-400` on `bg-gray-900`.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code and LLM dark themes generally (1); "systematically generate inaccessible markup" per CHI 2025 research cited in 12, 15.
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 1, 12, 15, 16
- **Quotes:** "Generated dark themes routinely ship body text that fails WCAG AA" (1).
- **Counter-evidence:** Impeccable files low contrast under "quality issue," not AI slop — humans do it constantly (16).

### T7. Unchanged slate/zinc neutrals (shadcn default palette)
- **What it looks like:** Muted indistinguishable grey surfaces identical to every other shadcn app.
- **In code:** default `--primary`, slate/zinc scale, unmodified shadcn CSS variables; `data-radix` attributes with default tokens (11).
- **Tools named:** shadcn/ui defaults; AI agents that "see shadcn, and reach for shadcn defaults on every new component" (5); v0, Lovable, Claude Code (1, 11).
- **Category:** colour / components
- **Sources:** 1, 5, 11
- **Quotes:** "Slate and zinc are the most common shadcn defaults, and they are the reason every shadcn app looks the same" (5). "Zero customisation, means nobody made a single visual decision" (11). "The library is explicitly designed to be copy-pasted by AI agents" (1).
- **Counter-evidence:** shadcn itself is fine — every source recommends customizing tokens, not abandoning the library.

### T8. Cream/beige as the "tasteful" escape hatch
- **What it looks like:** Warm cream or beige background used reflexively when the model tries to avoid the dark-purple cliché.
- **In code:** `bg-[#faf7f2]`-style backgrounds, warm off-whites everywhere.
- **Tools named:** general AI output (16).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 16
- **Quotes:** "A warm cream or beige background has become the default 'tasteful' AI surface, reached for by reflex" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Warm neutrals are a staple of good human editorial design; only the reflexive use is the tell.

### T9. Neon everything (oversaturated multi-hue palettes)
- **What it looks like:** Electric blue + hot pink + acid green at full saturation competing simultaneously.
- **In code:** multiple saturated Tailwind 400/500 hues used as coequal accents.
- **Tools named:** vibe-coding output generally; "modern SaaS UI" training data (7).
- **Category:** colour
- **Sources:** 7, 10
- **Quotes:** "Pick one dominant color, one accent, and one neutral. Everything else is noise" (7).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T10. Inter (and the Inter-family look) everywhere
- **What it looks like:** Inter as the only typeface, all weights, especially in a centered hero headline; no pairing, no hierarchy personality.
- **In code:** `font-family: Inter, sans-serif`; Tailwind `font-sans` default left untouched.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code, Cursor, v0, Windsurf (1, 2); shadcn/Tailwind default chain (5); AI sites generally (3, 6, 10, 11, 12, 16).
- **Category:** typography
- **Sources:** 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 16
- **Quotes:** "Inter is a wonderful typeface. It has also become the Helvetica of the LLM era" (1). "An unchosen Inter signals nobody made a typography decision" (3). "Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk converge on AI-generated UIs and no longer feel distinctive" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Explicit: Inter is high quality and ubiquitous in excellent human products (1, 3) — the tell is Inter *by default*, not Inter itself. saaslandingpage.com catalogs 200 human SaaS sites using Inter.

### T11. The recurring "AI font combos": Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Geist, DM Sans
- **What it looks like:** Same second-tier pairings appearing across unrelated generated sites; Space Grotesk headings, Geist body, Instrument Serif accents.
- **In code:** Google Fonts imports of exactly these families.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code, Cursor (1).
- **Category:** typography
- **Sources:** 1, 16
- **Quotes:** "The same font combos over and over: Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, and Geist" (1).
- **Counter-evidence:** These are real, well-made typefaces used by human designers; frequency is the tell.

### T12. Italic-serif accent word in an otherwise sans hero
- **What it looks like:** One word of the headline swapped to an italic serif ("Ship *beautiful* products"), or a full oversized italic serif display headline.
- **In code:** `<span class="font-serif italic">` inside an Inter H1.
- **Tools named:** LLM design tools generally (1, 16).
- **Category:** typography
- **Sources:** 1, 16
- **Quotes:** "Oversized italic serif as primary hero headline has become the universal AI-startup landing page hero" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** This was a human editorial/branding trend (c. 2023) before AI amplified it — practitioners note it "no longer feels distinctive" rather than never having been good (16).

### T13. Uppercase eyebrow/kicker label above the H1
- **What it looks like:** Tiny tracked all-caps label ("— HOW IT WORKS", "AI-POWERED") or pill chip directly above every heading.
- **In code:** `text-xs uppercase tracking-widest text-muted-foreground` above `<h1>`/`<h2>`.
- **Tools named:** default AI SaaS hero template (16); Claude Code, Cursor (1).
- **Category:** typography / layout
- **Sources:** 1, 16
- **Quotes:** "A tracked uppercase label above a heading borrows editorial authority it has not earned" (16). "A tiny uppercase letter-spaced label above an oversized headline is the default AI SaaS hero" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Kickers/eyebrows are a legitimate editorial device from print journalism; ubiquity plus emptiness is the tell.

### T14. Badge/pill directly above the hero H1
- **What it looks like:** Small rounded pill with sparkle emoji or "New ✨ v2.0 is live →" sitting right above the headline; repeated pill badges in every section ("badge spam").
- **In code:** shadcn `<Badge>` component before the `<h1>`; `rounded-full border px-3 py-1 text-sm`.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code, Cursor (1); vibe-coded apps generally (19).
- **Category:** components / layout
- **Sources:** 1, 16, 19
- **Quotes:** "Badge positioned right above the hero H1 appears on nearly every generated site" (1). "One or two badges can help orientation. Too many make it feel auto-generated" (19).
- **Counter-evidence:** The announcement pill is a Linear/Vercel human convention; it's the default-ness and repetition that signals AI.

### T15. Centered hero, confident headline, two buttons
- **What it looks like:** Symmetric centered hero: eyebrow, big headline, one-line subhead, primary+secondary button pair, optional screenshot below.
- **In code:** `text-center max-w-2xl mx-auto`, flex row of two buttons (`<Button>` + `<Button variant="outline">`).
- **Tools named:** Lovable, v0, Claude Code, Bolt, Claude Design — "Different companies. Different models. The same face." (4); LLM defaults (1, 10, 13).
- **Category:** layout
- **Sources:** 1, 4, 10, 11, 13, 16
- **Quotes:** "Centered hero set in a generic sans" (1). "Centered hero with confident headline and two buttons" (4).
- **Counter-evidence:** This is also the dominant human SaaS layout of the last decade — Shin et al. note convergence predates AI via Bootstrap/Tailwind (22); by itself a weak tell.

### T16. Three feature cards in a row (the triptych reflex)
- **What it looks like:** Exactly three equal cards with rounded corners, soft shadow, thin-line icon, heading, two lines of text. Sometimes a six-card 3×2 version.
- **In code:** `grid grid-cols-3 gap-4` / `md:grid-cols-3`; each card `flex flex-col items-center rounded-lg shadow`.
- **Tools named:** Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf (2); v0, Lovable via shadcn blocks (1, 11); all AI UI builders (12, 15).
- **Category:** layout
- **Sources:** 1, 2, 3, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 19
- **Quotes:** "Breaking the three-card reflex with asymmetry does more to de-slop a page than almost anything" (3). "Six identical cards in a row, each with an icon, a heading, and two lines" (15). "Replace 'feature grid' thinking with 'user problem' thinking" (19).
- **Counter-evidence:** Three-column feature rows are a decades-old human SaaS pattern (Bootstrap era); Shin et al. (22) — a weak tell alone, strong in combination.

### T17. Icon tile stacked above heading (the universal feature-card template)
- **What it looks like:** Small rounded-square container holding a line icon, centered above the card heading.
- **In code:** `w-10 h-10 rounded-lg bg-primary/10 flex items-center justify-center` wrapping a Lucide icon.
- **Tools named:** shadcn default patterns (1); universal AI template (16).
- **Category:** components
- **Sources:** 1, 12, 15, 16
- **Quotes:** "A small rounded-square icon container above a heading is the universal AI feature-card template" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T18. The full SaaS page skeleton in fixed order
- **What it looks like:** Hero → logo strip → 3 feature cards → how-it-works (1-2-3) → testimonials → stats → pricing → FAQ → CTA. Always this order, regardless of product.
- **In code:** Section components generated in exactly that sequence; bento grid variant (11).
- **Tools named:** all vague-prompt generation (13); template layout sign (11).
- **Category:** layout
- **Sources:** 11, 13, 16
- **Quotes:** "Hero section, three feature cards, testimonials, pricing, CTA. The result is fast, but generic" (13).
- **Counter-evidence:** It's also the standard human landing-page playbook; the tell is that nothing deviates and no section reflects the actual product (13, 19).

### T19. Numbered 1-2-3 step sequences and tiny numbered section labels
- **What it looks like:** "How it works" with numbered circles; or tiny editorial numbers ("01", "02") beside headings.
- **In code:** `<ol>` with circle counters; `01/` prefixes styled `text-sm text-muted-foreground`.
- **Tools named:** LLM templates (1); generated-UI signature (16).
- **Category:** layout
- **Sources:** 1, 16
- **Quotes:** "Tiny numbers beside headings imitate editorial structure without adding it" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T20. Stat banner rows with fake-feeling metrics
- **What it looks like:** Horizontal row: "10K+ users · 99.9% uptime · 50% faster" — big number, small label, gradient accent — on products with no users yet.
- **In code:** `flex justify-around` stat cards; CountUp animations.
- **Tools named:** LLM design defaults (1); "hero metric layout" (16); fake product evidence (19).
- **Category:** layout / copy
- **Sources:** 1, 16, 19
- **Quotes:** "Big number, small label, three supporting stats, gradient accent. Used everywhere, trusted nowhere" (16). "If you are early, say less and show more" (19).
- **Counter-evidence:** Real stats presented this way are fine; the tell is invented numbers.

### T21. Cards for everything, cards inside cards
- **What it looks like:** Every content block wrapped in a bordered rounded container; nesting 2-3 levels deep; excessive visual weight.
- **In code:** shadcn `<Card>` wrapping `<Card>`; `rounded-xl border bg-card p-6` at every level.
- **Tools named:** model "applies container logic without cost function for visual weight" (7).
- **Category:** layout / components
- **Sources:** 7, 16, 19
- **Quotes:** "Use whitespace, proximity, and typography for grouping. Reserve cards for genuinely interactive content" (7). "Cards inside cards create visual noise and excessive depth" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T22. Coloured accent border on card edge (side-tab stripe)
- **What it looks like:** 3-4px coloured stripe on the left (or top) edge of cards/callouts, often a different colour per card with no meaning.
- **In code:** `border-l-4 border-purple-500`, `border-t-4`.
- **Tools named:** LLM design loops (1); vibe-coded UIs (7); "the most recognizable tell of AI-generated UIs" (16).
- **Category:** components
- **Sources:** 1, 7, 16, 19
- **Quotes:** "Colored left borders are almost as reliable a sign of AI-generated design as em-dashes" (1). "The model conflates accent colors for active states with need for universal emphasis" (7).
- **Counter-evidence:** With semantic meaning (severity, status) it's a legitimate pattern — "Reserve for status/severity; use consistently with meaning" (19).

### T23. Rounded corners on everything, at the same middle radius
- **What it looks like:** Every card, input, button, image softened to the same safe radius; or over-rounded into "the same soft blob".
- **In code:** `rounded-lg`/`rounded-2xl` everywhere; shadcn `--radius: 0.5rem` untouched; 8px/16px dominance (14).
- **Tools named:** shadcn defaults (5); Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf (2); AI mobile UIs (14).
- **Category:** components
- **Sources:** 2, 5, 6, 10, 14, 16
- **Quotes:** "The default radius variable is somewhere around half a rem. That is the safe middle" (5). "Over-rounding cards, sections, and inputs rounds everything into the same soft blob" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Rounded corners are universal in modern human design; uniform unconsidered radius is the tell, and fix guidance is directional (0px, 1rem, or pill — just choose) (5).

### T24. Glassmorphism as decoration
- **What it looks like:** Frosted-glass cards, blur panels, glow borders used everywhere rather than to solve a layering problem; often combined with neon glow.
- **In code:** `backdrop-filter: blur()`, `bg-white/10 backdrop-blur border border-white/20`.
- **Tools named:** LLM residual of the 2022 trend (1); all AI UI builders (12); slopdar's gradient-aesthetic cluster (11).
- **Category:** components
- **Sources:** 1, 11, 12, 15, 16
- **Quotes:** "Glassmorphism is the frosted-glass card treatment that had a moment in 2022 and has been LLM default" (1). "Blur effects, glass cards, and glow borders used as decoration rather than to solve a real layering problem" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Deliberate glass layering (e.g., macOS-style) is respected human design; decorative overuse is the tell (16).

### T25. Hairline border + wide diffuse shadow on the same element
- **What it looks like:** Cards with both a 1px border and a big soft shadow — two conflicting elevation systems at once.
- **In code:** `border border-gray-200 shadow-xl`.
- **Tools named:** recurring generated-UI signature (16); "each component uses a different shadow recipe" (23).
- **Category:** components
- **Sources:** 16, 23
- **Quotes:** "A hairline border paired with a wide, diffuse shadow is a recurring generated-UI signature" (16). "Depth cues behave like decoration because each component uses a different shadow recipe" (23).
- **Counter-evidence:** None found.

### T26. Decorative grid / dot-matrix backgrounds
- **What it looks like:** Faint graph-paper grid or dot pattern behind hero sections with no functional meaning.
- **In code:** `background-image: linear-gradient(...)` grid lines or radial dot patterns at low opacity; repeating-gradient stripes variant.
- **Tools named:** generated-UI signature (16).
- **Category:** imagery
- **Sources:** 16
- **Quotes:** "A decorative grid covers a surface without supporting a canvas, map, or measurement task" (16).
- **Counter-evidence:** Human dev-tool brands (Vercel, Linear) popularized grid backgrounds; another amplified human trend.

### T27. Emoji as UI icons
- **What it looks like:** Emoji in navigation, sidebars, feature cards, section headers, bullet lists instead of an icon system.
- **In code:** raw Unicode emoji inside `<nav>`/headings; no SVG icon library.

## Fixes practitioners recommend

- **Write a DESIGN.md / design brief before prompting** — 20-50 lines covering palette, type, spacing, radius, forbidden patterns; keeps agents consistent across sessions (1, 12, 24). "The model does what it's told. You have to do the telling first" (12).
- **Cap the palette** — one dominant, one accent, one neutral (7); max 4 values: background, surface, primary, text (12); make secondary colour "the absence of color" (5).
- **Replace defaults with tokens** — customize shadcn `--primary`, `--radius`, neutral scale; "add one token shadcn does not include by default… that one extra token gives the project a fingerprint" (5); define 3-5 named elevation levels mapped to shared tokens (23).
- **Name an actual font pairing** — distinctive display + refined body; avoid unchosen Inter; e.g. "DM Sans 700 headings" specified explicitly (3, 5, 12, 16, 24).
- **Explicitly forbid the clichés in prompts** — "no purple gradients, no Inter, no centered hero, no three cards" (10, 24); Anthropic's own frontend-design skill warns against "cliched color schemes (particularly purple gradients on white backgrounds)" (8).
- **Give references, not adjectives** — name reference sites (Stripe, Vercel), aesthetics ("1970s ski lodge," "Japanese print design"), 3-5 Dribbble/Mobbin/Awwwards references with notes on why they work (6, 10, 24).
- **Break the triptych with asymmetry** — asymmetric layouts, varied card sizes, content that resists a neat 3-up (3, 12, 16).
- **Use real product evidence** — screenshots of actual workflow instead of icon grids and invented stats; "specific language beats polished language" (19).
- **Enforce accessibility gates** — WCAG AA / APCA contrast checks, focus states, sequential headings, state coverage (hover/focus/disabled/error/loading) before shipping (1, 12, 15, 23).
- **Closed-loop critique** — iterate → critique → fix → re-evaluate against an acceptance checklist rather than one-shot generation (15); "step away from iterative AI refinement for projects requiring precision" (4).
- **Plan taste separately from code** — decide creative direction in text first; explore layouts with image models (FLUX, Ideogram, Recraft) before a coding agent implements (24). "Generic is what one prompt does when it has to pick taste, explore, and write code all at once" (24).
- **Motion with intent** — GSAP for scroll, Motion.dev for UI state, timelines planned before implementation; animate transform/opacity only; motion shows state, never proves the site is alive (16, 19, 24).
- **Assign personas / constraints in system prompts** — "senior frontend engineer" personas, `<frontend_aesthetics>` wrappers from the Anthropic Cookbook, curated design-guideline system prompts (9, 10).

## Things practitioners say are NOT reliable tells (false positives)

- **Any single sign in isolation.** Slopdar is explicit: "Every sign above also appears on plenty of hand-built sites" — detection requires weighted aggregates, and refined AI-built sites score low (11).
- **Inter itself.** "Inter is a wonderful typeface" (1); hundreds of respected human SaaS products use it. Only *unchosen* Inter signals no typography decision (3).
- **Purple gradients per se.** Humans invented the look: Stripe, Instagram, Twitch (2015-2020) (8); Notion, Linear, Vercel (7). AI amplified an existing human trend.
- **Dark mode.** Legitimate when depth comes from typography and surface levels rather than glows (7).
- **The Next.js/Vercel/Supabase stack and Tailwind classes** — the most popular human indie stack too (11, 18). Builder fingerprints (Framer/Webflow meta tags) prove a builder, not AI (18).
- **Centered heroes, 3-column feature rows, rounded corners, minimalist sans layouts** — Shin et al. document that this convergence predates AI via Bootstrap/Tailwind and industry pressure toward frictionless efficiency (22); these are weak signals alone (1, 16 treat them as signals only in combination).
- **Polish/fluency itself.** Kakehi's core point: AI design is not ugly or broken — it is *too* fluent; you cannot spot it by looking for mistakes (4).
- **Craft defects (low contrast, tiny text, cramped padding, justified text).** Impeccable deliberately labels these "quality issues," common in human work, distinct from AI-slop signatures (16).
- **Em-dashes and kickers/eyebrows** — standard professional writing/editorial devices; only density and emptiness signal AI (16).
