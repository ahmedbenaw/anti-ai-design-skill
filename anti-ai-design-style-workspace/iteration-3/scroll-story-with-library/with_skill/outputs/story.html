How we make it

Olive oil from one press in the Siwa oasis, Egypt. Five steps, from the tree to the bottle in your kitchen.

01

Where the trees grow

Siwa is an oasis in Egypt's Western Desert, close to the Libyan border. Its water comes from springs, not the Nile. The soil is sandy and holds a lot of salt.

Olive trees like this. The days are hot and dry, the nights are cool, and the trees are rarely wet. Our groves sit between the date palms, in the shade they give.

02

When we pick

We pick in autumn, when the fruit has turned from green to purple but is not yet black. Earlier gives a sharper, greener oil. Later gives more oil but a flatter taste. We go for the middle.

The fruit comes off by hand and with small rakes, onto nets under the tree. Nothing is picked up off the ground. Bruised fruit goes into a separate crate and does not become our oil.

03

How we press

The olives are pressed on the same day they are picked. A day's wait in the heat is enough to start the fruit turning, and you can taste it.

We wash the fruit, crush it whole with the stones in, and stir the paste slowly. The paste is kept under 27 degrees the whole time. Then the oil is spun out of the paste. No heat, no chemicals, nothing added.

"Cold pressed" on our label means this: the paste never goes above 27 degrees Celsius.
04

Before it is bottled

Fresh oil is cloudy. We let it rest in closed steel tanks so the fine bits settle to the bottom on their own. We draw the clear oil off the top and leave the rest behind.

It goes into dark glass, because light spoils oil faster than time does. Every bottle carries the month it was pressed. Keep it closed, cool and out of the sun, and use it within a year.

05

Where the press is

The press is in the town of Siwa, near the old fortress of Shali. Ask us before you come, and we will show you round.
