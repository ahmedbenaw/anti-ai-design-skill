Siwa Oasis, Egypt

How we make it

Five steps, one press, and a few hundred very old trees.

Scroll down

01 — The grove

It starts with the trees

Our olives grow in the Siwa Oasis, a low green basin in the Western Desert about fifty kilometres from the Libyan border. The trees drink from natural springs, not the Nile. Many of them are older than anyone can remember, and most are the local Siwi variety, a small, dense olive that has adapted to the salt and the heat.

Nothing is sprayed. The desert does most of the pest control for us.

02 — The harvest

Picked by hand, in October

Harvest begins when the fruit is turning from green to purple. Families climb the trees with rakes and combs and let the olives fall onto cloth spread beneath. It is slow work, and we keep it that way, because bruised fruit begins to ferment before it reaches the press.

Everything picked in the morning is at the press by the afternoon.

03 — The press

Washed, crushed, and pressed the same day

At the press the olives are sorted, rinsed with spring water, and crushed whole, stone and all, into a thick paste. The paste is worked gently for a short while so the oil droplets can find each other, then separated in a cold process. We never let the temperature climb, which is why the oil keeps its green, peppery bite.

04 — The rest

Settled in the dark

Fresh oil is cloudy and a little wild. We let it stand in steel tanks in a cool room for a few weeks so the fine sediment sinks on its own. No filters, no additives. What rises is the oil; what settles stays behind.

05 — The bottle

Bottled small, sent out fresh

We bottle in dark glass, in small runs, and label each batch with its harvest month. Oil is best in its first year, so we would rather sell out than sit on stock. If you open a bottle and it smells like cut grass and green tomato, it has arrived the way it left us.

Where to find us

The press in Siwa

The press sits on the edge of town, on the road toward the salt lakes. Visitors are welcome during the harvest season.
