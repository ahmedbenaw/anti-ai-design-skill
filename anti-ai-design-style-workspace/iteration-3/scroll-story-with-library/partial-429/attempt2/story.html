How We Make It

Olive oil from the Siwa Oasis, Egypt

One

The grove

Siwa sits in a hollow of the Western Desert, three hundred kilometres from the sea. The olives grow between date palms, watered by springs that have run since before anyone kept records.

Most of our trees are the old Siwi variety. They are slow, small-fruited, and used to the heat.

Two

The harvest

We pick from late October into November, by hand and with small rakes, onto nets laid under the trees. Nothing is shaken from the branches.

The fruit is picked when it has just started to turn, still mostly green. That is when the oil is at its most peppery.

Three

The press

Olives go to the press the same day they are picked. They are washed, the leaves are blown off, and the fruit is crushed to a paste.

The paste is worked gently for about half an hour, then the oil is separated from the water and the pulp. We keep everything cool. No heat, no solvents, no second pressing.

Four

The rest

Fresh oil is cloudy. We let it settle in steel tanks for a few weeks, out of the light, so the sediment drops on its own.

We taste each tank before it is bottled. If it is not right, it does not go out under our name.

Five

The bottle

We bottle in dark glass, in small runs, with the harvest year on the label. Oil is best in its first year, so we would rather you finish a bottle than keep it.

What is left over goes back to the grove: the pomace as compost, the leaves and prunings as mulch.

Where to find us

The press is in the Siwa Oasis, Matrouh Governorate, Egypt. Visitors are welcome during the harvest; write ahead and we will set a time.
